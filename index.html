Method & limitations

Required gross floor area = units × net unit size ÷ efficiency. Replacement capacity proxy = sum of lot area × PLUTO residential FAR, not unused FAR or legal entitlement.

Accommodation is capped at 1 for scoring. Removal and assembly use inverse min-max normalization across the complete multi-lot candidates in this snapshot; a constant component ties at 1. Scores are weighted sums × 100, not feasibility percentages. Equal scores share rank.

The detached Dutch Kills creek waterfront does not face Roosevelt Island. It is excluded from the shoreline before generating the 1,000-foot candidate buffer, not just hidden from the map.

All existing residential units are presumed removed. Occupancy, displacement and existing affordability are unknown. Net gains assume the full target is built, not a forecast. Retention adds/removes zero units.

Park conflicts remain visible, not excluded. Zoning, MIH, ownership, approvals, costs and engineering are unresolved. Tax-lot assemblages are not verified legal zoning lots.

This is a mode-neutral redevelopment screen, shared with no-bridge redevelopment. No pedestrian/bike, transit or vehicle bridge benefit has been calculated.

Equations, assumptions & interpretation

Lower-case r, p, s and c are ratios to separate rent, value, stock and annual-flow references. The table/diagram multiply these ratios by 100. At default common parameters and with no intervention, all four indices equal 100.

  1. Space demand: r = d × s−1/ε. ε is demand elasticity magnitude; d = 1 + assumed bridge willingness-to-pay shift for bridge scenarios, otherwise 1.
  2. Asset value: p = r / k, where k is the cap-rate multiplier. NOI is assumed proportional to rent with an unchanged operating-income margin; no separate expenses, concessions or vacancy changes are estimated.
  3. Development: c = (p / q)η + a. q is a delivery-cost multiplier; η is construction-response elasticity. a is the sustained additional net delivery in redevelopment scenarios, as a fraction of reference annual delivery, otherwise zero. This power curve has no hard feasibility threshold, land constraint or zoning cap.
  4. Stock balance: c = ℓ × s in long-run equilibrium. ℓ multiplies an unspecified reference loss rate. In physical units St+1 = St + Ct − Lt; reference annual delivery C₀ = δ₀S₀. Neither δ₀ nor actual years/square feet are supplied.

Construction here means net additions after project-specific removals, before background losses. Extra delivery is exogenous and assumed funded/deliverable; it is not a finding that redevelopment is profitable. Endogenous market construction can fall when added supply lowers rents. A one-off development should not be interpreted as a permanent annual increment.

The no-intervention scenario shares the current cap-rate, cost and loss assumptions. It may therefore differ from reference 100. Short-run redevelopment alone changes no stock yet; short-run values reflect rent/capitalization only, without anticipation of future supply. This is not a time-path or investment forecast.

Increasing supply can moderate rent relative to the bridge-only scenario without necessarily returning it to the no-intervention level. A bridge-demand shift is assumed, may be zero or negative, and is not inferred from the Connectivity walking graph. No district boundaries, units, affordability rules, stabilized rents, site capacity, costs, subsidy or 5,000-unit delivery target are modeled.

Equations & what “optimal” means

F = gross residential floor area / lot area. Housing price per gross residential square foot is P(F) = α − βF; average non-land development cost is C(F) = μ + γF. Land residual per lot square foot is L(F) = F[P(F) − C(F)] = (α − μ)F − (β + γ)F². Costs must include all non-land development costs and required return for an appraisal interpretation; here they are illustrative only.

Because β > 0 and γ ≥ 0, the residual is strictly concave. Without a ceiling, F* = max(0, (α − μ) / [2(β + γ)]). Under a hypothetical ceiling F̄ ≥ 0, the chosen F = min(F*, F̄), allowing no new building at F = 0 with zero development residual. If α ≤ μ, no positive development residual exists. Existing-use land value is not assumed zero; it is simply excluded from this new-development calculation.

The positive price/cost crossing is F₀ = (α − μ) / (β + γ), if α > μ. It has zero residual, not maximum residual. At the interior optimum, P + FP′ = C + FC′ (marginal total revenue equals marginal total cost); generally P is greater than average C there. For these linear curves, F* = F₀ / 2. When α = μ the only nonnegative crossing is zero; when α < μ there is none.

The price slope summarizes an assumed density/quality/amenity tradeoff, not a claim that increasing FAR always lowers sale prices. Floor area and cost denominators are consistently gross residential area; rentable area, efficiency, unit mix and nonresidential uses are not modeled. Raising a ceiling above F* alone does not raise residual value in this model.

Demolition, acquisition of existing buildings, displacement, affordable-unit restrictions, taxes, vacancy, financing timing, bridge costs, engineering and approval risks are not separately estimated. Residual is not a market appraisal, net profit or proof of feasibility. Nothing changes Siting FAR inputs, rankings, the four-quadrant assumptions or the 5,000-unit baseline.
